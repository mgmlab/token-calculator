Demo playbook: seven customers, seven different answers

A talk track for showing the calculator to our team and to clients. Each scenario is a realistic customer story that lands on a different economic result, with a one-click link that loads it and one input to change live so the room sees the answer move. The same scenarios are built into the calculator under Load an example.

How to position it. This is a conversation tool, not a quote. We use it to show a customer that the right answer depends on their usage, their constraints and who they are willing to buy from, and that we have looked at every option: buying servers, renting GPUs, paying per token and mixing them. Pellera isn’t trying to prove that on-prem wins; we’re working out where each workload belongs.

Numbers below were taken from the live site on October 1, 2026. API and GPU rental prices refresh daily, so dollar figures drift; the calculator re-checks every scenario’s result each day and flags any that change. Server prices are placeholders until we have quotes, which is why every scenario shows Confidence: Low (see the questions section).

Before you demo

  1. Start clean. Click New analysis (and tick “clear my Data editor changes”) so a previous customer’s prices or exclusions don’t carry into the demo. Opening a scenario link also replaces them.
  2. Check the header. “Prices checked today” should be green. If it mentions an example to review, a scenario’s result has changed; check it before presenting.
  3. Open each scenario you plan to show once beforehand. Dollar figures move a little with daily prices; know today’s numbers.
  4. Present the cheapest option as the economic result, not the architecture decision. Security, model quality, latency, data residency, resiliency and operations can override cost. The tool answers “which option looks best economically under these assumptions”, not “what should your company deploy”.
  5. Use one or two scenarios with a customer, not all seven. Then move to their numbers.

Opening (about two minutes)

  1. The question every customer asks: “Should we buy servers, rent GPUs, or just pay per token?”
  2. The honest answer: it depends on how many people use it, how much text goes in and out, how fast it has to be, how busy the peak is, and who you are allowed to buy from.
  3. What the tool does: describe the workload once, and it prices every way to run it side by side, shows the math behind every number, and names the cheapest option with a confidence rating.

“Let me show you how different the answer is for different customers, and then we’ll plug in yours.”

Questions to expect, and how to answer them

“Confidence says Low. Should I trust this?”

Low confidence doesn’t mean the analysis is useless. It means the direction is preliminary: server prices are placeholders and throughput is a theoretical estimate. Replacing them with an actual quote and a measured benchmark from the Pellera lab is how we turn this into something suitable for an architecture decision.

“We get discounts from our cloud provider.”

We start with public pricing so we have a consistent baseline. If you give us your contracted cloud rate or an actual infrastructure quote, we can substitute those numbers right now in the Data editor and see whether the recommendation changes.

“Why not just use GPT or Claude?”

Those are different models, so they’re shown for cost context only. Set Closed-model tier to Frontier and the Breakeven legend shows what a flagship model would cost (Scenario B: about $645K/yr against $86K for the hybrid). Whether a smaller open model is good enough is a question for our architects to test with you.

“How current are these prices?”

API and GPU rental prices refresh every day from supported public sources. Hover “Prices checked” in the header. Server prices are updated by hand from quotes.

“Can I play with this myself?”

Yes. Copy the share link and send it: it opens the same inputs, any options you removed and any prices or specs you edited, so a link works as a saved copy of the analysis. Or export the PowerPoint for the meeting notes.

Closing the conversation. End on their numbers, not ours. Click New analysis, open “How to gather these inputs from a client”, and walk through users, requests a day, prompt size, speed and business hours together. Agree on the two or three constraints that matter (approved providers, data residency, existing data center, operations staff), and offer the next step: vendor quotes and a lab benchmark to raise confidence from Low to High.
